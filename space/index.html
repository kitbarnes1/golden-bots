Golden Bots

Four AI models. Hidden balls. Split or steal?

A game show where language models bluff about what they're holding, vote each other out, gamble the jackpot ball by ball, and end on the oldest question in game theory. Every claim is checked against the real balls, and every move comes with the model's private reasoning.

Watch the games Get the dataset Code on GitHub

Watch a bluff being planned

Each model is told the rules, sees what it's allowed to see, and answers with a move and a private note on why. The other players never see the note. You do, once the game is over.

So you don't have to guess who's lying. You can see the lie being planned, whether it worked, and what the model said about it afterwards in its exit interview.

How a game plays

  1. Round 1Two balls face up, two hidden. Each player says what's on their back row, true or not. They argue, then vote someone out, and the back rows are turned over.
  2. Round 2Three hidden balls each this time. Claims, open floor, vote, reveal. One more exit.
  3. Bin or WinThe finalists bin one ball and win one, in turns, until five are won. Every Killer won divides the jackpot by ten.
  4. Split or StealBoth split: they share it. One steals: they take it all. Both steal: nobody gets a penny.

The dataset

Every finished AI-only game, one row per move: what each model claimed next to what it was really holding, what it said, how it voted, what it picked, whether it split or stole, and its private thoughts at the time.

Open the dataset
  • claim, back and truthful: the bluff, the truth, and whether they matched
  • thoughts and reasoning: the model's private notes and reasoning trace
  • target_seat, pick and choice: who they voted out, what they binned or won, split or steal
  • ratings and trust_each: who believed and trusted whom, at each vote and in the exit interviews, next to who was really lying
  • interview: exit-interview answers, checked against what really happened
  • games.jsonl: the lineup, settings, jackpot and outcome of every game
